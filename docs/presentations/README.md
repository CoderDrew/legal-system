# MatterMind Product Decision Deck

## Purpose

This presentation shows how MatterMind stakeholder discovery led to a defensible MVP product decision. It explains why active-matter status reconstruction was selected, what V1 includes and excludes, and which assumptions subsequent feasibility and validation work must address. It is retained as a record of the product-decision phase.

The deck is a product strategy and MVP scope artifact. It does not define a technical architecture, select an AI provider, or commit to specific integration or retrieval technologies.

## Intended audience

- Law-firm leadership evaluating the business rationale and operating boundaries.
- Product and technical team members preparing for validation and feasibility work.
- Portfolio reviewers assessing the transition from stakeholder discovery to an evidence-based AI product decision.

## Narrative

The 12-slide story:

1. Introduces MatterMind and the product-decision phase.
2. Separates established discovery findings from unresolved assumptions.
3. Shows the three candidate workflows investigated during discovery.
4. Explains why prospective-client intake was reserved rather than selected.
5. Explains why deadlines and follow-up were deferred.
6. Presents the case for active-matter status reconstruction.
7. Defines the conceptual V1 trigger, input, reconstruction behavior, and output.
8. Illustrates a synthetic Matter Status Brief experience.
9. Defines evidence states and the read-only trust model.
10. Establishes explicit V1 scope boundaries.
11. Summarizes the assumptions and invalidation conditions behind the decision.
12. Identifies the product questions that must be validated next.

## Design decisions

- A restrained navy, blue, amber, and warm-white palette matches the existing MatterMind discovery visual.
- Generated illustrations appear only where they clarify the product story; they do not represent real people, matters, or events.
- Product flows, evidence states, scope boundaries, and the Matter Status Brief are editable PowerPoint objects.
- The deck uses large type, generous whitespace, and short evidence-based statements for presentation readability.
- Established findings and unresolved assumptions use separate visual treatments.
- The deck deliberately ends with validation questions rather than architecture.
