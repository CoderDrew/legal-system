# Discovery Interview 004 — Paralegal: Active-Matter Status

## Interview record

- **Stakeholder:** Karen Mitchell, Paralegal
- **Organization:** Fictional law firm
- **Experience:** Approximately seven years with the firm
- **Interview date:** Not provided
- **Objective:** Investigate the question “Where are we on this matter?” by understanding how a frontline employee determines the current state of an active matter, where the necessary information resides, how much work reconstruction requires, and what happens when the reconstructed state is incomplete or incorrect.
- **CONSTRAINT:** This interview remains distinct from the earlier intake-workflow discovery.

## Stakeholder role and tools

- **FACT:** Karen supports several attorneys, including David Chen.
- **FACT:** She works primarily on employment and business matters.
- **FACT:** Her common tools include Clio, Outlook, Microsoft Word, Microsoft Teams, and Microsoft 365.

## Example matter

- **OBSERVATION:** Karen demonstrated the process using an active matter referred to as the Henderson matter.
- **FACT:** David had focused on another case for much of the week and asked Karen where the Henderson matter stood.
- **OBSERVATION:** Karen demonstrated how she reconstructed the answer.

## Starting with Clio

- **OBSERVATION:** Karen first opened the Henderson matter in Clio.
- **OBSERVATION:** She reviewed recent notes, open tasks, upcoming calendar items, and matter activity.
- **OBSERVATION:** Clio provided part of the matter's current state.
- **OBSERVATION:** Karen found a response deadline scheduled for the following week, a related task, and the most recent note entered by David.
- **FACT:** Karen stated that Clio did not necessarily show everything that had happened recently.

## Outlook investigation

- **FACT:** Karen knew there had been recent communication with opposing counsel.
- **OBSERVATION:** She switched from Clio to Outlook.
- **FACT:** Karen finds that combining the client's name and the opposing attorney's name narrows search results more effectively than searching only for the client.
- **OBSERVATION:** She used that combination to search Outlook.
- **OBSERVATION:** She located an email thread with opposing counsel containing approximately eleven messages across several days.
- **OBSERVATION:** Karen read the thread to reconstruct what had occurred.
- **OBSERVATION:** She determined that opposing counsel had requested an extension, David had agreed to it, and opposing counsel subsequently sent additional documents.
- **OBSERVATION:** Karen checked Clio and found that the changed deadline had been updated.
- **OBSERVATION:** She did not find a corresponding Clio note describing the newly received documents.

## Additional client communication

- **OBSERVATION:** Karen returned to Outlook and found a separate email from the client.
- **OBSERVATION:** The client had supplied additional information related to material in the opposing party's documents.
- **OBSERVATION:** Karen reviewed the client communication and an attachment.

## Microsoft 365 documents

- **OBSERVATION:** Karen opened a Word document associated with the matter in Microsoft 365.
- **OBSERVATION:** She checked the latest version of a draft response to understand its current state.
- **OBSERVATION:** At this point, determining matter status had required Karen to consult Clio, Outlook, email attachments, and Microsoft 365 documents.

## Reconstructed matter state

- **OBSERVATION:** Karen reconstructed that opposing counsel requested an extension and the firm agreed to it.
- **OBSERVATION:** The new deadline was the following Friday.
- **OBSERVATION:** Opposing counsel produced additional documents on Tuesday.
- **OBSERVATION:** The client provided responsive information on Wednesday.
- **OBSERVATION:** The response draft was being updated.
- **OBSERVATION:** The next internal step was waiting for David to review the latest draft.
- **FACT:** Karen summarized the underlying problem as, “It's usually not in one place.”

## Existing process for updating Clio

- **FACT:** The firm has processes for recording important information in Clio.
- **FACT:** Important emails are expected to be saved back to the matter when appropriate.
- **FACT:** If something changes the matter, such as a deadline, the appropriate task or calendar entry should be updated.
- **FACT:** Notes may be added when information needs to be preserved for others.
- **CONSTRAINT:** The evidence must not be characterized as showing that the firm has no process for maintaining Clio.
- **OBSERVATION:** The issue Karen described is that maintaining complete matter context in Clio requires additional human effort while the underlying work occurs elsewhere.

## Work outside Clio

- **FACT:** Karen stated that Outlook is where much of the actual communication occurs.
- **FACT:** An attorney in an active email exchange would need to interrupt that work to open Clio, find the correct matter, save the appropriate email, potentially create a note, update affected tasks, statuses, or dates, and then return to Outlook.
- **FACT:** Karen identified ambiguity about what should be preserved from an email thread.
- **FACT:** For an eleven-message thread, unresolved choices include whether to save all messages, only the final message, the extension request, the response agreeing to the extension, or the message containing documents.
- **FACT:** Karen or assistants may later clean up information in Clio when time permits.
- **FACT:** Some attorneys are more disciplined about this than others.
- **CONSTRAINT:** These findings must not be characterized as simple user resistance or failure to adopt Clio.

## Critical information and complete history

- **OBSERVATION:** The changed deadline in the example matter had been updated in Clio.
- **FACT:** Karen explained that deadline information is important enough that someone made sure it was recorded.
- **FACT:** She stated that the firm cannot rely on someone remembering a changed deadline from an email.
- **FACT:** Karen distinguished recording critical information from reproducing every detail of Outlook activity inside Clio.
- **FACT:** She stated that staff do not have time to recreate the entire Outlook mailbox in Clio.
- **OBSERVATION:** The interview indicates a distinction between business-critical structured state that must be maintained and contextual matter history that may remain distributed across working systems.
- **CONSTRAINT:** This distinction is not yet a technical design decision.

## Frequency of matter-state questions

- **FACT:** Karen distinguished formal summaries from questions that effectively require summarization.
- **FACT:** She is not constantly asked to produce formal matter summaries.
- **FACT:** Questions that amount to asking for current matter state occur regularly.
- **FACT:** Examples include “What's happening with Henderson?”, “Did we ever hear back from opposing counsel?”, whether the team is still waiting for something, what happens next, and client inquiries about current status.
- **FACT:** Karen may need to piece together the status of several matters during a normal week.
- **FACT:** In some weeks, this may occur daily.
- **CONSTRAINT:** These frequencies are stakeholder estimates, not measured usage data.

## Who needs matter state

- **FACT:** Karen identified attorneys, paralegals, legal assistants, and herself as people who may need current-state information.
- **FACT:** Clients may need the information indirectly through firm staff.
- **FACT:** Karen may need it after returning to a matter she has not touched recently.
- **FACT:** Attorneys frequently ask these questions, particularly after focusing on other work for several days.

## Variation by matter complexity

- **FACT:** Karen emphasized that many matter-state questions are easy.
- **FACT:** If she knows a matter well, she may answer from memory or check Clio briefly.
- **FACT:** More difficult cases include active matters with significant email traffic, documents moving between parties, multiple people working on the matter, or a period during which the person answering has not worked on it recently.
- **FACT:** These situations require more deliberate reconstruction.

## Time required

- **FACT:** Karen estimated that a matter like Henderson may require approximately 10 minutes to reconstruct.
- **FACT:** She estimated that a very active matter she has not worked on recently may require 15–20 minutes.
- **FACT:** Some matters may require longer.
- **CONSTRAINT:** These durations are stakeholder estimates and have not been independently measured.
- **CONSTRAINT:** They must not be used as established return-on-investment calculations without further evidence.

## Information that changes the answer

- **FACT:** Karen stated that she sometimes initially believes she knows the answer but later finds an email that changes her understanding.
- **OBSERVATION:** The matter-state problem is therefore not solely about convenience or memory; information in another system may materially change the answer.

## Meaning of current matter state

- **FACT:** To answer “Where are we on this matter?” confidently, Karen wants to know the last important event, what the firm is waiting on, what must happen next, whether there is a deadline, what significant new information recently arrived, and who owns the next step.
- **FACT:** Possible next-step owners include the client, opposing counsel, an attorney, a paralegal, or other firm staff.
- **FACT:** Karen does not necessarily need the entire matter history.
- **FACT:** When an attorney asks where a matter stands, the attorney generally needs enough information to resume work effectively.
- **CONSTRAINT:** Karen's description defines useful matter-state information; it is not a finalized MatterMind feature set.

## Risk of incorrect matter state

### Deadline risk

- **FACT:** Karen identified deadlines as the most concerning category.
- **FACT:** Missing or misunderstanding a deadline could have serious consequences.

### Operational risk

- **FACT:** Incomplete matter state could cause duplicate work or unnecessary follow-up.
- **FACT:** Work could proceed without awareness of recently received information.
- **FACT:** Staff could contact opposing counsel about something already answered.

### Client experience

- **FACT:** The firm could provide outdated information to a client.
- **FACT:** A client could know about a development that firm staff have not incorporated into their understanding of the matter.
- **FACT:** Karen stated that this can make the firm appear disorganized.

### Verification behavior

- **FACT:** Karen does not like answering from memory when she is uncertain.
- **FACT:** She would rather spend time checking the relevant systems before answering.

## Trust and source verification

- **FACT:** Karen said she would not trust a system that simply stated the current status if she could not determine where the information came from.
- **FACT:** If a system says the firm is waiting on opposing counsel, Karen wants to know which email, note, document, or other source supports that conclusion.
- **FACT:** Karen expressed particular concern about deadline information.
- **FACT:** If a deadline is presented, she wants to see the source from which it was derived.
- **FACT:** Karen's business need includes source verification and traceability.
- **CONSTRAINT:** This need must not be prematurely translated into retrieval-augmented generation, embeddings, vector search, a citation architecture, or a particular AI provider.
- **CONSTRAINT:** Those remain possible technical approaches rather than discovery findings.

## Direct observations

- **OBSERVATION:** Determining the current state of an active matter may require information from multiple systems.
- **OBSERVATION:** Clio contains important structured matter state but may not contain all recent contextual information.
- **OBSERVATION:** Outlook contains communications that may materially change the understanding of a matter.
- **OBSERVATION:** Microsoft 365 documents may contain additional evidence about work currently in progress.
- **OBSERVATION:** Employees perform manual synthesis across these sources when matter context is no longer fresh.
- **OBSERVATION:** Critical information such as deadlines receives stronger process attention than general matter context.
- **OBSERVATION:** Maintaining complete contextual information in Clio carries a human labor cost.
- **OBSERVATION:** Not every matter requires extensive reconstruction; complexity increases with activity volume, distributed information, multiple participants, and time away from the matter.
- **OBSERVATION:** Source verification is important when presenting reconstructed matter state.

## Hypotheses requiring validation

- **HYPOTHESIS:** The active-matter problem may be less about lack of Clio adoption and more about the cost of synchronizing work occurring across Clio, Outlook, and Microsoft 365.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** Providing a concise, source-grounded representation of current matter state could reduce manual reconstruction effort.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** A useful current-state representation may focus on recent significant events, current blockers or waiting state, next action, owner, deadlines, and significant new information rather than the entire matter history.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** Matter-state assistance may be most valuable on high-activity matters rather than uniformly across all matters.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** Source traceability may be necessary for user trust and safe adoption.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** Deadline-related information may require stronger verification and deterministic controls than general contextual summaries.

These hypotheses have not been validated and must not be treated as requirements.

## Cross-interview evidence

- **FACT:** Rachel Morgan identified difficulty answering “Where are we on this matter?” as one of three recurring complaint areas.
- **FACT:** David Chen independently stated that returning to an active matter after several days can require reconstructing what happened, especially after substantial email activity.
- **OBSERVATION:** Karen demonstrated that reconstruction process during Interview 004.
- **OBSERVATION:** Legal-operations leadership, an attorney, and a paralegal have now provided evidence that active-matter context reconstruction is a workflow concern.
- **CONSTRAINT:** The workflow must not be assumed to be identical across all practice groups, attorneys, paralegals, or matter types.

## Emerging cross-workflow pattern

- **OBSERVATION:** Discovery has examined prospective-client intake, initial attorney intake review, and active-matter status reconstruction.
- **OBSERVATION:** Across those workflows, important information has been found in Clio, Outlook, Microsoft 365 documents, Teams, SharePoint, and human memory or institutional knowledge.
- **OBSERVATION:** Employees compensate for boundaries between these sources manually.
- **OBSERVATION:** Examples include copying website information into Clio, reviewing documents for conflict-check parties, using Outlook categories for intake ownership, consulting a SharePoint attorney-availability spreadsheet, supplementing Clio tasks with Teams or email, searching Outlook for matter developments, reviewing Microsoft 365 documents for work state, and checking whether Clio reflects recent activity.
- **CONSTRAINT:** This emerging pattern does not establish that MatterMind should become a unified information layer or cross-system AI assistant.
- **CONSTRAINT:** Such a conclusion would be a product or architecture decision that has not been made.

## Product boundaries

- **CONSTRAINT:** Do not assume that putting everything into Clio is necessarily the solution.
- **FACT:** Karen confirmed that the firm already has processes for maintaining important information in Clio.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** Completely duplicating all working information from Outlook and Microsoft 365 into Clio may impose impractical maintenance costs.
- **CONSTRAINT:** Do not assume that MatterMind should become another system of record.
- **CONSTRAINT:** Clio is staying, and the firm does not want another independently maintained source of truth.
