# Discovery Interview 003 — Employment Attorney

## Interview record

- **Stakeholder:** David Chen, Employment Attorney
- **Organization:** Fictional law firm
- **Role:** Senior associate in the employment practice
- **Interview date:** Not provided
- **Objective:** Follow the intake workflow across the boundary between intake staff and an attorney. Understand what David receives, reviews, retrieves, performs, records, and communicates before deciding the next step; identify where practice differs from the expected Clio workflow and what information would make attorney review easier.
- **CONSTRAINT:** This interview does not expand into the broader active-matter workflow. David's related comment is recorded only as a lead for future discovery.

## Stakeholder role and tools

- **FACT:** David receives prospective employment matters from the intake team.
- **FACT:** He reviews submitted information and documents.
- **FACT:** He determines whether additional information is needed.
- **FACT:** He decides whether a matter should proceed to consultation.
- **FACT:** He hands appropriate next steps to support staff.
- **FACT:** His common tools include Clio, Outlook, Microsoft Word, Microsoft Teams, and the Microsoft 365 environment.

## Intake handoff

- **FACT:** Maria's intake workflow formally hands prospective matters to attorneys through Clio.
- **FACT:** David receives a Clio task informing him that a prospective matter is ready for review.
- **OBSERVATION:** The Clio task functions as the formal intake-to-attorney handoff in the workflow David described.
- **FACT:** David opens the prospective matter in Clio and reviews the information recorded by intake.
- **FACT:** This information includes basic prospective-client information and the intake description.

## Reviewing original material

- **FACT:** David does not rely solely on the intake summary.
- **FACT:** He wants to review what the prospective client actually submitted.
- **FACT:** Depending on how intake was processed, relevant documents may be associated with the prospective matter, or David may need to locate the original intake material through Outlook or another linked location.
- **OBSERVATION:** In the example workflow, the prospective client had supplied multiple documents.
- **OBSERVATION:** David reviewed those documents in addition to the information recorded by intake.
- **FACT:** David's need to review original material does not imply distrust of the intake coordinator.
- **FACT:** The intake coordinator and attorney perform different jobs and therefore require different levels of understanding.

## Attorney review goals

- **FACT:** During initial review, David is trying to understand what happened, when it happened, who was involved, and what the prospective client wants.
- **FACT:** He also considers potentially important deadlines, available documentation, whether the issue is potentially a matter the firm handles, and what information is missing.
- **OBSERVATION:** In the example matter, the prospective client alleged termination after making a complaint to management.
- **OBSERVATION:** The submitted material included correspondence with the employer and a termination letter.
- **OBSERVATION:** David reconstructed the sequence of events from the intake information and supporting documents.
- **FACT:** David described this process as getting the story straight before deciding whether the matter warrants a consultation.

## Time spent reviewing intakes

- **FACT:** David estimated that reviewing a simple prospective matter may take approximately 5–10 minutes.
- **FACT:** He estimated that a more complicated intake involving a long narrative and multiple documents may take approximately 20 minutes or longer.
- **FACT:** David may have several prospective matters waiting for review while he is also working on active client matters.
- **CONSTRAINT:** These durations are stakeholder estimates and have not been independently measured.
- **CONSTRAINT:** The estimates must not be converted into quantified return-on-investment calculations without additional evidence.

## Decision: proceed to consultation

- **OBSERVATION:** For the example reviewed during the interview, David determined that the matter warranted a consultation.
- **OBSERVATION:** He updated the prospective matter in Clio.
- **OBSERVATION:** The workflow then required another human handoff for scheduling.
- **FACT:** David's assistant commonly handles consultation scheduling.
- **FACT:** David can create or update a task indicating that a consultation should be scheduled.
- **FACT:** David acknowledged that he sometimes communicates this directly to his assistant through another channel, such as Teams.
- **FACT:** An example of that communication is, “Can you get this person on my calendar?”
- **OBSERVATION:** Clio provides the formal workflow, but Teams or other direct communication may become the practical workflow for some handoffs.
- **OBSERVATION:** This is consistent with Maria's description of sometimes supplementing Clio tasks with Teams or email.

## Decision: additional information needed

- **FACT:** David may determine that he cannot yet decide whether the matter should proceed.
- **FACT:** In that situation, he may request additional information.
- **FACT:** The expected workflow can involve recording a note or creating or updating a task in Clio so intake knows what is required.
- **FACT:** David may instead communicate the request directly to Maria through email or Teams.
- **OBSERVATION:** Relevant workflow state can therefore exist outside Clio.

## Decision: do not proceed

- **FACT:** David may decide that the firm should not proceed with a prospective matter.
- **FACT:** Reasons can include the matter being outside the firm's practice area, the firm not handling that type of issue, capacity considerations, or other matter-specific reasons.
- **CONSTRAINT:** This interview does not establish a complete decline-decision policy.
- **FACT:** David stated that the primary workflow issue is how the decision is recorded and communicated, rather than his ability to make the decision.
- **FACT:** If the appropriate Clio status, field, note, or task is not updated, intake may still see what appears to be a pending prospective matter.
- **FACT:** When the prospective client later contacts the firm, intake may need to investigate the status manually.
- **OBSERVATION:** This aligns with Maria's report that she sometimes contacts an attorney or assistant to determine what happened after handoff.

## Attorney pain point: information comprehension

- **OBSERVATION:** David's primary pain point differs from Maria's reported pain points.
- **FACT:** Maria emphasized manual information transfer and intake coordination.
- **FACT:** David spends time understanding potentially lengthy submissions before deciding what requires deeper review.
- **FACT:** David described the problem approximately as: “I'd love to stop reading ten pages just to figure out whether I need to read ten pages.”
- **FACT:** The meaning David conveyed is that he would benefit from quickly understanding the structure and key contents of a prospective matter before deciding where detailed review is necessary.
- **FACT:** Information David indicated would be useful includes what the prospective client says happened, important people and organizations, an apparent chronology or timeline, documents supplied, dates that may matter, and information that appears to be missing.
- **CONSTRAINT:** These are stakeholder information needs, not a finalized product feature list.

## Trust and source verification

- **FACT:** David said he would not trust a generated summary by itself.
- **FACT:** When information is presented as coming from a source document, David wants to verify it against that source.
- **FACT:** Ideally, he would be able to move directly from a statement or extracted fact to the underlying supporting material.
- **FACT:** David's business need is source verification and traceability.
- **CONSTRAINT:** This need must not be prematurely translated into a specific implementation such as retrieval-augmented generation, vector search, embeddings, a citation architecture, or a particular AI model.
- **CONSTRAINT:** Those are possible future implementation choices, not discovery findings.

## Related active-matter lead

- **FACT:** David stated that a similar information-reconstruction problem occurs with active matters.
- **FACT:** When returning to an active matter after several days, particularly one with substantial email activity, he may spend time determining what has happened and where the matter currently stands.
- **OBSERVATION:** This aligns with Rachel Morgan's previously identified concern: “Where are we on this matter?”
- **CONSTRAINT:** This subject was not explored further during Interview 003.
- **OPEN QUESTION:** How does the active-matter “Where are we?” problem differ from the intake-review problem?

## Cross-interview findings

- **OBSERVATION:** The described intake workflow can now be followed across these roles and transitions: prospective client → intake → conflict review → attorney assignment → attorney review → decision → scheduling, additional information, decline, or other follow-up.
- **FACT:** Maria and David independently described Clio as the formal workflow mechanism.
- **FACT:** Both also described situations in which Teams, email, or direct communication supplements the formal process.
- **FACT:** Maria reported having to investigate prospective-matter status when Clio does not reflect what happened after attorney handoff.
- **FACT:** David independently described situations in which he may make a decision but communicate it through a channel other than the expected Clio workflow.
- **OBSERVATION:** These accounts provide corroborating evidence that workflow state can become fragmented across systems and communication channels.
- **CONSTRAINT:** This behavior must not be generalized to every attorney, practice group, or matter without further validation.

## Direct observations

- **OBSERVATION:** The formal intake-to-attorney handoff occurs through Clio.
- **OBSERVATION:** Attorney review requires a deeper understanding of prospective-client material than intake processing requires.
- **OBSERVATION:** David may consult Clio, Outlook, and submitted documents to reconstruct the prospective matter.
- **OBSERVATION:** Attorney review involves synthesizing information across structured intake data and unstructured documents.
- **OBSERVATION:** Clio is sometimes supplemented by Teams or email during workflow transitions.
- **OBSERVATION:** Information about a decision may therefore exist outside the system expected to represent workflow state.
- **OBSERVATION:** Maria and David independently described the consequences of incomplete post-handoff status information.
- **OBSERVATION:** David wants source verification when information is summarized or extracted for him.

## Hypotheses requiring validation

- **HYPOTHESIS:** Providing attorneys with a concise, source-grounded overview of prospective-matter submissions could reduce the time required to determine what deserves deeper review.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** A useful matter overview may include parties, chronology, potentially important dates, supplied documents, and missing information.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** Improving synchronization between attorney decisions and Clio workflow state could reduce status-chasing by intake staff.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** Some intake workflow problems may be deterministic workflow or integration problems rather than AI problems.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** Some document-comprehension problems may be appropriate candidates for AI assistance.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** The distinction between workflow automation and information comprehension may become important when defining an eventual MVP.

These hypotheses have not been validated and must not be treated as requirements.

## Important boundaries

- **CONSTRAINT:** Do not infer that attorneys want AI making intake-acceptance decisions.
- **FACT:** David described a need for faster comprehension, not autonomous legal decision-making.
- **CONSTRAINT:** Do not infer that David wants to stop reading source documents.
- **FACT:** David explicitly indicated that source verification is important.
- **CONSTRAINT:** Do not characterize Clio as failing or being rejected by users.
- **OBSERVATION:** The evidence indicates that Clio is the formal workflow system while some real-world communication and work occurs outside it.
- **CONSTRAINT:** Do not assume that every attorney follows David's workflow.
- **FACT:** David represents one employment attorney; further validation may be required.

## Emerging pattern — not yet a product decision

Across Interviews 001–003, two broad categories are beginning to emerge.

### Category A: Workflow and deterministic-process concerns

- **OBSERVATION:** Examples observed so far include intake ownership, transfer of structured website information into Clio, attorney assignment, task or status updates, notifications, and workflow state communicated through multiple channels.

### Category B: Information-comprehension concerns

- **OBSERVATION:** Examples observed so far include identifying possible parties within submitted documents, understanding lengthy submissions, reconstructing chronology, identifying potentially important dates, understanding which documents were supplied, identifying missing information, and verifying extracted or summarized information against original sources.

- **CONSTRAINT:** These categories are discovery observations only.
- **CONSTRAINT:** No decision has been made that either or both categories belong in an MVP.
- **CONSTRAINT:** No decision has been made that Category A requires AI.
- **CONSTRAINT:** No decision has been made that Category B requires a specific AI architecture.
- **CONSTRAINT:** MatterMind must not be assumed to replace Clio.
- **CONSTRAINT:** MatterMind must not be assumed to become a separate system of record.
- **CONSTRAINT:** The existing discovery boundary remains that Clio is staying and the firm does not want another independently maintained system of record.
